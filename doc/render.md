# Rendering

The engine's render data in `src/engine/render_*.odin`: the draw list, the camera and the primitive meshes. `render_webgl.odin` is the WebGL2 renderer that reads them in the browser.

## The draw list

`Draw_List` carries a `Camera`, a sun (`Directional_Light`: direction, colour, intensity), the `exposure` the tone map applies (1 keeps the scene as rendered), the hemisphere colours `sky_colour` and `ground_colour` the ambient term blends between, the draw items, each a mesh id, a material id and a transform matrix, and the point lights (`Point_Light`: position, colour, intensity, and the range where the light reaches zero; the intensity is what a white Lambert surface one metre away, facing the light, returns). Demos and modules fill it every frame with `draw_list_add` and `draw_list_add_light`, empty it with `draw_list_clear` (which keeps the capacity) and free it with `draw_list_destroy`. The renderer only reads it.

## Conventions

Metres, y up, right handed, OpenGL clip space (`core:math/linalg` with its `flip_z_axis` default, near maps to -1 and far to 1), counter clockwise front faces seen from outside. A `Camera` holds its position, target and up vector, the vertical field of view in radians and the near and far planes. `view_matrix` and `projection_matrix` (given the aspect) build its matrices. `orbit_camera` places one around a target from an `Orbit`: yaw about y measured from +z, pitch up from the xz plane, and the distance, with up along y. Orbit input works through two pure steps: a drag turns the yaw and pitch (pitch limited to `ORBIT_PITCH_LIMIT`, short of the poles) in `orbit_drag`, and a pinch or wheel zoom scales the distance within a range in `orbit_zoom`. Two fingers or the right button move the target in the view plane through `orbit_pan`, scaled by `orbit_metres_per_pixel` so a pixel of drag moves the target a pixel at its depth. WASD and the arrow keys walk the target along the ground and E and Q move it up and down through `orbit_walk`, at a speed proportional to the orbit distance. A gamepad in the standard mapping does the same: the left stick walks, the right stick turns, the triggers zoom and the d-pad moves up and down, the sticks through `stick_dead_zone` so a resting stick drifts nothing.

## Primitive meshes

`box_mesh(size)` is centred at the origin: six faces of four vertices carrying the face normal, 24 vertices and 36 indices. `cylinder_mesh(radius, height, segments)` runs along y, centred at the origin: two side rings of `segments + 1` vertices (the seam vertex repeated) with radial normals, and two caps of one centre and `segments` ring vertices with the cap normal, so `4 * (segments + 1)` vertices and `12 * segments` indices. `room_mesh(size)` is a `box_mesh` turned inside out, its normals negated and its triangles wound the other way, so its faces point inward: drawn from inside, culled from outside. All three return `Mesh_Data` (vertices of position and normal, `u32` indices), freed with `mesh_data_destroy`.

## Materials

`data/materials/library.sjson` is the material library every demo and module shares (embedded with `#load`), one object per material with the keys `albedo` (three numbers, 0 to 1), `roughness` (0 to 1), `metallic` (0 to 1), the optional `emissive` (three numbers, 0 or more, default zero), the optional `textures` (a string, the texture set of `doc/workflows/materials.md`) and the optional `wear` (`edge_width` in metres, `chip_threshold` 0 to 1, `amount` 0 or more, `metal` three numbers 0 to 1, the wear rule of the same document). Albedo is the paint or surface colour for a dielectric (`metallic = 0`) and the reflectance colour for a metal (`metallic = 1`). `parse_material_library` in `src/engine/material.odin` reads it into a `Material_Library` and rejects an unknown key, a missing key and a value out of range with a problem naming the material and the key, and `material_library_destroy` frees it. The `Material_Id` of a material is its index in alphabetical order of name, because the parsed SJSON object is a map without order and the ids must not change from one run to the next. `material_id_by_name` looks one up.

## Scene files

A demo's scene lives in `data/demos/<name>/scene.sjson` with the keys `camera` (an `Orbit`: `target`, `yaw`, `pitch`, `distance`), `sun` (`direction`, `colour`, `intensity`), `exposure`, the optional `ambient`, the flat ambient level (0.15 when absent), the optional `sky_colour` and `ground_colour` (three numbers each, `ambient` times white when absent), `items` (an array of objects, each with a `shape`, `position` and `material` name, plus `size` for a `"box"` or a `"room"` (a box whose faces point inward) or `radius`, `height` and `segments` for a `"cylinder"`) and the optional `lights` (an array of point lights: `position`, `colour`, `intensity`, `range`). `parse_scene` in `src/engine/scene.odin` reads it against the `Material_Library` into a `Scene`, and `scene_destroy` frees it. Every key is required unless noted, and an unknown key (a key of another shape included), a material not in the library, `segments` below 3 or not an integer, a negative `distance`, `intensity`, `range`, `exposure`, `ambient`, `radius` or `height`, a negative component of `sky_colour` or `ground_colour`, and an all zero sun direction are rejected with a problem naming the key and the item or light index. `scene_item_mesh` gives an item's `Mesh_Data` by shape. `Scene_Page` in `src/engine/web_scene_page.odin` (web only) draws a scene on a canvas with the orbit input: `scene_page_init` uploads the meshes and listens, `scene_page_step` builds the draw list and draws.

## The WebGL2 renderer

`src/engine/render_webgl.odin` carries `#+build js wasm32`, so it belongs to the `engine` package for the browser target only. `webgl_renderer_init` compiles and links four GLSL ES 3.00 programs, the forward one from `src/engine/shaders/forward.vert` and `forward.frag` and the fullscreen ones, which share `tonemap.vert`, with `downsample.frag`, `upsample.frag` and `tonemap.frag`, loaded with `#load` and validated by `./build.sh check`, enables `EXT_color_buffer_float` (needed before a half float colour attachment renders), and enables the depth test (`LEQUAL`) and back face culling with counter clockwise front faces. `webgl_renderer_upload_mesh` uploads a `Mesh_Data` once into a vertex array (attribute 0 the position, attribute 1 the normal, `u32` indices) and returns its `Mesh_Id`, the index in upload order. `webgl_renderer_draw` runs a forward pass, the bloom passes and the tone map pass. The forward pass renders into a `Webgl_Target`, an `RGBA16F` colour texture (linear filtering so the first bloom downsample averages two by two texels, clamped) with a 24 bit depth renderbuffer, which `webgl_target_ensure` recreates whenever the canvas size changes; it clears the target and issues one draw call per item of the `Draw_List`, skipping an item whose mesh or material id is out of range, and writes linear colour, so values above 1 survive. The post passes each draw one fullscreen triangle (from `gl_VertexID`, no vertex attributes) with the depth test off. The bloom is a lens scattering term after Jimenez 2014, with no threshold: a chain of `WEBGL_BLOOM_LEVELS` (5) targets (linear filtering, no depth), level 0 half the canvas and each next level half the previous, floored at one pixel. A 13 tap downsample fills level 0 from the scene colour and each next level from the one before it: the centre, a 3 by 3 ring two source texels apart and an inner square one texel out, combined as four overlapping corner boxes weighted 0.125 each and the inner box weighted 0.5. On level 0 each box is further weighted by `1 / (1 + luma)` (Rec. 709 luma) and the sum divided by the sum of the weights, the partial Karis average, so a single bright pixel does not flicker. Then, from the smallest level up, a 3 by 3 tent upsample (weights 1 2 1, 2 4 2, 1 2 1 over 16, one source texel apart) of each level is added into the next larger one with additive blending, so level 0 ends up holding all levels. The tone map pass draws to the canvas: it divides level 0 by `WEBGL_BLOOM_LEVELS`, since a flat scene arrives once per accumulated level, and mixes the target towards that by `WEBGL_BLOOM_STRENGTH` (0.05), so the picture keeps its brightness, scales the result by the draw list's `exposure`, maps it through the ACES fit of Narkowicz 2015, `x (2.51 x + 0.03) / (x (2.43 x + 0.59) + 0.14)` clamped to 0 to 1, and applies the gamma 2.2 output. When the target is not framebuffer complete the draw only clears the canvas. The shading is physically based: a GGX specular lobe with the Smith geometry term in its Schlick-GGX form and Schlick Fresnel (`F0` 0.04 for dielectrics, the albedo for metals, mixed by `metallic`), and a Lambert diffuse term scaled by the light the Fresnel term does not reflect and by `1 - metallic`, plus the material's emissive. Roughness is floored at 0.045, where GGX would otherwise degenerate. The sun's intensity is defined so that a white Lambert surface facing it returns the intensity, so the radiance is `sun_colour * π`. The fragment shader runs at `highp`, since the squared alpha at the roughness floor sits below mediump's normal range. The ambient term is the albedo times the draw list's `ground_colour` and `sky_colour` blended by the normal's height, `mix(ground_colour, sky_colour, n.y * 0.5 + 0.5)`, so a face pointing straight up gets the sky colour and one pointing straight down the ground colour, until the sky model of stage 1 feeds the colours. Up to `WEBGL_MAX_POINT_LIGHTS` (eight) point lights shade each fragment, the rest of the list is ignored, each with an inverse square falloff windowed to reach zero at its range, `clamp(1 - (d / range)^4, 0, 1)^2 / max(d^2, 0.01)`. The sun direction is the direction the light travels, towards the scene, so a sun overhead points down. The normal is transformed with `mat3(model)`, which assumes a uniform scale in the model matrix.
