# Rendering

The engine's render data in `src/engine/render_*.odin`: the draw list, the camera and the primitive meshes. `render_webgl.odin` is the WebGL2 renderer that reads them in the browser.

## The draw list

`Draw_List` carries a `Camera`, a sun (`Directional_Light`: direction, colour, intensity), the `exposure` the tone map applies (1 keeps the scene as rendered), the hemisphere colours `sky_colour` and `ground_colour` the ambient term blends between, the draw items, each a mesh id, a material id and a transform matrix, and the point lights (`Point_Light`: position, colour, intensity, and the range where the light reaches zero; the intensity is what a white Lambert surface one metre away, facing the light, returns). Demos and modules fill it every frame with `draw_list_add` and `draw_list_add_light`, empty it with `draw_list_clear` (which keeps the capacity) and free it with `draw_list_destroy`. The renderer only reads it.

## Conventions

Metres, y up, right handed, OpenGL clip space (`core:math/linalg` with its `flip_z_axis` default, near maps to -1 and far to 1), counter clockwise front faces seen from outside. A `Camera` holds its position, target and up vector, the vertical field of view in radians and the near and far planes. `view_matrix` and `projection_matrix` (given the aspect) build its matrices. `orbit_camera` places one around a target from an `Orbit`: yaw about y measured from +z, pitch up from the xz plane, and the distance, with up along y. `Fly` is the first person camera, made from an orbit by `fly_from_orbit` and turned and walked by `fly_turn` and `fly_walk`, its camera from `fly_camera`. Orbit input works through two pure steps: a drag turns the yaw and pitch (pitch limited to `ORBIT_PITCH_LIMIT`, short of the poles) in `orbit_drag`, and a pinch or wheel zoom scales the distance within a range in `orbit_zoom`. Two fingers or the right button move the target in the view plane through `orbit_pan`, scaled by `orbit_metres_per_pixel` so a pixel of drag moves the target a pixel at its depth. WASD and the arrow keys walk the target along the ground and E or Space moves it up and Q, Shift or Control down through `orbit_walk`, at a speed proportional to the orbit distance. A gamepad in the standard mapping does the same: the left stick walks, the right stick turns, the triggers zoom and the d-pad moves up and down, the sticks through `stick_dead_zone` so a resting stick drifts nothing.

## Primitive meshes

`box_mesh(size)` is centred at the origin: six faces of four vertices carrying the face normal, 24 vertices and 36 indices. `cylinder_mesh(radius, height, segments)` runs along y, centred at the origin: two side rings of `segments + 1` vertices (the seam vertex repeated) with radial normals, and two caps of one centre and `segments` ring vertices with the cap normal, so `4 * (segments + 1)` vertices and `12 * segments` indices. `room_mesh(size)` is a `box_mesh` turned inside out, its normals negated and its triangles wound the other way, so its faces point inward: drawn from inside, culled from outside. All three return `Mesh_Data` (vertices of position and normal, `u32` indices), freed with `mesh_data_destroy`.

## Materials

`data/materials/library.sjson` is the material library every demo and module shares (embedded with `#load`), one object per material with the keys `albedo` (three numbers, 0 to 1), `roughness` (0 to 1), `metallic` (0 to 1), the optional `emissive` (three numbers, 0 or more, default zero), the optional `textures` (a string, the texture set of `doc/workflows/materials.md`) and the optional `wear` (`edge_width` in metres above 0, `chip_threshold` 0 to 1, `amount` 0 or more, `metal` three numbers 0 to 1, the wear rule of the same document). Albedo is the paint or surface colour for a dielectric (`metallic = 0`) and the reflectance colour for a metal (`metallic = 1`). `parse_material_library` in `src/engine/material.odin` reads it into a `Material_Library` and rejects an unknown key, a missing key and a value out of range with a problem naming the material and the key, and `material_library_destroy` frees it. The `Material_Id` of a material is its index in alphabetical order of name, because the parsed SJSON object is a map without order and the ids must not change from one run to the next. `material_id_by_name` looks one up.

## Scene files

A demo's scene lives in `data/demos/<name>/scene.sjson` with the keys `camera` (an `Orbit`: `target`, `yaw`, `pitch`, `distance`, plus the optional `fit_width`, the width in metres the start view shows across the canvas, and on a narrower canvas the page moves the camera back through `orbit_fit_width`, within the zoom's maximum, the models demo naming one and the three others none), `sun` (`direction`, `colour`, `intensity`), `exposure`, the optional `ambient`, the flat ambient level (0.15 when absent), the optional `sky_colour` and `ground_colour` (three numbers each, `ambient` times white when absent), `items` (an array of objects, each with a `shape`, `position` and `material` name, plus `size` for a `"box"` or a `"room"` (a box whose faces point inward) or `radius`, `height` and `segments` for a `"cylinder"`, or `file` (a mesh blob's file name) and the optional `scale` (above 0, a uniform scale, 1 when absent) for a `"mesh"`) and the optional `lights` (an array of point lights: `position`, `colour`, `intensity`, `range`), and the optional `labels` (an array of plaques: `text`, `position`, the point the plaque stands above). `parse_scene` in `src/engine/scene.odin` reads it against the `Material_Library` into a `Scene`, and `scene_destroy` frees it. Every key is required unless noted, and an unknown key (a key of another shape included), a material not in the library, `segments` below 3 or not an integer, a negative `distance`, `fit_width`, `intensity`, `range`, `exposure`, `ambient`, `radius` or `height`, a `scale` of 0 or less, a negative component of `sky_colour` or `ground_colour`, and an all zero sun direction are rejected with a problem naming the key and the item, light or label index. `scene_item_mesh` gives an item's `Mesh_Data` by shape, a mesh item's by `parse_mesh_blob` (`src/engine/render_mesh_blob.odin`) of its file among the mesh files passed in, with a problem naming a missing file. The blob format (`BAMESH01`, the counts, the vertices, the indices) is the one of `doc/workflows/models.md`, and `parse_mesh_blob` rejects a wrong magic, a length that does not match the counts and an index at or past the vertex count. `scene_item_half_extents` gives a mesh item the half extents of the smallest box centred on the origin that holds its parsed mesh (`mesh_half_extents`), unscaled, since the shader's edge distance reads the vertex position before the scale, and so a blob standing on the origin gets edge wear at its top rim and none at its unseen bottom. `Scene_Page` in `src/engine/web_scene_page.odin` (web only) draws a scene on a canvas with the orbit input: `scene_page_init` takes the texture and the mesh blob directories (each through `#load_directory`, `nil` for a demo without meshes), uploads the meshes, keeping each item's half extents, and listens, `scene_page_step` builds the draw list, each item drawn at its position times its uniform scale, and draws. The labels are HTML elements, since the runtime has no DOM writer: `scene_page_init` registers the page (`scene_page_register`), and the contextless exports `label_count`, `label_text_pointer`, `label_text_length`, `label_screen_x`, `label_screen_y` and `label_visible` give `web/labels.js` each label's text and its point in CSS pixels by `world_to_screen` (`src/engine/render_camera.odin`) with the current orbit's camera, visible when in front of the camera and within the view.

## The WebGL2 renderer

`src/engine/render_webgl.odin` carries `#+build js wasm32`, so it belongs to the `engine` package for the browser target only. `webgl_renderer_init` compiles and links four GLSL ES 3.00 programs, the forward one from `src/engine/shaders/forward.vert` and `forward.frag` and the fullscreen ones, which share `tonemap.vert`, with `downsample.frag`, `upsample.frag` and `tonemap.frag`, loaded with `#load` and validated by `./build.sh check`, enables `EXT_color_buffer_float` (needed before a half float colour attachment renders), and enables the depth test (`LEQUAL`) and back face culling with counter clockwise front faces. `webgl_renderer_upload_mesh` uploads a `Mesh_Data` once into a vertex array (attribute 0 the position, attribute 1 the normal, `u32` indices) and returns its `Mesh_Id`, the index in upload order. The texture sets are decoded at page load from the embedded `data/materials/textures/` directory by `texture_set_from_files` and uploaded by `webgl_renderer_upload_texture_set` with mipmaps (trilinear filtering), repeat wrapping, the rows flipped so t runs upward as the normal map's +Y assumes, and the albedo as sRGB, one `Webgl_Texture_Set` per material at its `Material_Id`, `present` false for a material without one. `webgl_renderer_draw` runs a forward pass, the bloom passes and the tone map pass. The forward pass renders into a `Webgl_Target`, an `RGBA16F` colour texture (linear filtering so the first bloom downsample averages two by two texels, clamped) with a 24 bit depth renderbuffer, which `webgl_target_ensure` recreates whenever the canvas size changes; it clears the target and issues one draw call per item of the `Draw_List`, skipping an item whose mesh or material id is out of range, and writes linear colour, so values above 1 survive. An item whose material has a texture set binds its three maps to the units 0 to 2 and samples them triplanar from the world position divided by `WEBGL_TEXTURE_METRES` (2): the projections along x, y and z (uv `p.zy`, `p.xz`, `p.xy`) are weighted by the geometric normal's `pow(abs(n), 4)` normalised to sum 1, and the albedo and surface maps mix this near scale half and half with a far scale e times larger on rotated axes (`p.zxy / e`, blend `.zxy`) so the period never shows; the albedo map is tinted by the preset's albedo, the surface map's red channel gives the roughness, and the normal map, near scale only, perturbs the normal by Golus's whiteout blend (2017). Such an item also applies the wear rule of `doc/workflows/materials.md`, with the surface map's blue channel at the two scales as the field and as the edge distance the second smallest of the face distances `d = half_extents - abs(local_position)`, `max(max(min(d.x, d. The edge distance is a box's: a cylinder gets its bounding box's, which would raise wear in four stripes on its side, so a textured cylinder waits for a baked curvature mask.y), min(d.x, d.z)), min(d.y, d.z))`, from the mesh's local position and the draw item's `half_extents`; a chip takes the preset's `metal` as albedo, the surface map's green channel as roughness and metallic 1. The post passes each draw one fullscreen triangle (from `gl_VertexID`, no vertex attributes) with the depth test off. The bloom is a lens scattering term after Jimenez 2014, with no threshold: a chain of `WEBGL_BLOOM_LEVELS` (5) targets (linear filtering, no depth), level 0 half the canvas and each next level half the previous, floored at one pixel. A 13 tap downsample fills level 0 from the scene colour and each next level from the one before it: the centre, a 3 by 3 ring two source texels apart and an inner square one texel out, combined as four overlapping corner boxes weighted 0.125 each and the inner box weighted 0.5. On level 0 each box is further weighted by `1 / (1 + luma)` (Rec. 709 luma) and the sum divided by the sum of the weights, the partial Karis average, so a single bright pixel does not flicker. Then, from the smallest level up, a 3 by 3 tent upsample (weights 1 2 1, 2 4 2, 1 2 1 over 16, one source texel apart) of each level is added into the next larger one with additive blending, so level 0 ends up holding all levels. The tone map pass draws to the canvas: it divides level 0 by `WEBGL_BLOOM_LEVELS`, since a flat scene arrives once per accumulated level, and mixes the target towards that by `WEBGL_BLOOM_STRENGTH` (0.05), so the picture keeps its brightness, scales the result by the draw list's `exposure`, maps it through the ACES fit of Narkowicz 2015, `x (2.51 x + 0.03) / (x (2.43 x + 0.59) + 0.14)` clamped to 0 to 1, and applies the gamma 2.2 output. When the target is not framebuffer complete the draw only clears the canvas. The shading is physically based: a GGX specular lobe with the Smith geometry term in its Schlick-GGX form and Schlick Fresnel (`F0` 0.04 for dielectrics, the albedo for metals, mixed by `metallic`), and a Lambert diffuse term scaled by the light the Fresnel term does not reflect and by `1 - metallic`, plus the material's emissive. Roughness is floored at 0.045, where GGX would otherwise degenerate. The sun's intensity is defined so that a white Lambert surface facing it returns the intensity, so the radiance is `sun_colour * π`. The fragment shader runs at `highp`, since the squared alpha at the roughness floor sits below mediump's normal range. The ambient term is the albedo times the draw list's `ground_colour` and `sky_colour` blended by the normal's height, `mix(ground_colour, sky_colour, n.y * 0.5 + 0.5)`, so a face pointing straight up gets the sky colour and one pointing straight down the ground colour, until the sky model of stage 1 feeds the colours. Up to `WEBGL_MAX_POINT_LIGHTS` (eight) point lights shade each fragment, the rest of the list is ignored, each with an inverse square falloff windowed to reach zero at its range, `clamp(1 - (d / range)^4, 0, 1)^2 / max(d^2, 0.01)`. The sun direction is the direction the light travels, towards the scene, so a sun overhead points down. The normal is transformed with `mat3(model)`, which assumes a uniform scale in the model matrix.
