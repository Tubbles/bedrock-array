# Rendering

The engine's render data in `src/engine/render_*.odin`: the draw list, the camera and the primitive meshes. `render_webgl.odin` is the WebGL2 renderer that reads them in the browser.

## The draw list

`Draw_List` carries a `Camera`, a sun (`Directional_Light`: direction, colour, intensity), the `exposure` the tone map applies (1 keeps the scene as rendered) and the draw items, each a mesh id, a material id and a transform matrix. Demos and modules fill it every frame with `draw_list_add`, empty it with `draw_list_clear` (which keeps the capacity) and free it with `draw_list_destroy`. The renderer only reads it.

## Conventions

Metres, y up, right handed, OpenGL clip space (`core:math/linalg` with its `flip_z_axis` default, near maps to -1 and far to 1), counter clockwise front faces seen from outside. A `Camera` holds its position, target and up vector, the vertical field of view in radians and the near and far planes. `view_matrix` and `projection_matrix` (given the aspect) build its matrices. `orbit_camera` places one around a target from an `Orbit`: yaw about y measured from +z, pitch up from the xz plane, and the distance, with up along y. Orbit input works through two pure steps: a drag turns the yaw and pitch (pitch limited to `ORBIT_PITCH_LIMIT`, short of the poles) in `orbit_drag`, and a pinch or wheel zoom scales the distance within a range in `orbit_zoom`.

## Primitive meshes

`box_mesh(size)` is centred at the origin: six faces of four vertices carrying the face normal, 24 vertices and 36 indices. `cylinder_mesh(radius, height, segments)` runs along y, centred at the origin: two side rings of `segments + 1` vertices (the seam vertex repeated) with radial normals, and two caps of one centre and `segments` ring vertices with the cap normal, so `4 * (segments + 1)` vertices and `12 * segments` indices. Both return `Mesh_Data` (vertices of position and normal, `u32` indices), freed with `mesh_data_destroy`.

## Materials

`data/materials/library.sjson` is the material library every demo and module shares (embedded with `#load`), one object per material with the keys `albedo` (three numbers, 0 to 1), `roughness` (0 to 1), `metallic` (0 to 1) and the optional `emissive` (three numbers, 0 or more, default zero). Albedo is the paint or surface colour for a dielectric (`metallic = 0`) and the reflectance colour for a metal (`metallic = 1`). `parse_material_library` in `src/engine/material.odin` reads it into a `Material_Library` and rejects an unknown key, a missing key and a value out of range with a problem naming the material and the key, and `material_library_destroy` frees it. The `Material_Id` of a material is its index in alphabetical order of name, because the parsed SJSON object is a map without order and the ids must not change from one run to the next. `material_id_by_name` looks one up.

## The WebGL2 renderer

`src/engine/render_webgl.odin` carries `#+build js wasm32`, so it belongs to the `engine` package for the browser target only. `webgl_renderer_init` compiles and links two GLSL ES 3.00 programs, the forward one from `src/engine/shaders/forward.vert` and `forward.frag` and the tone map one from `tonemap.vert` and `tonemap.frag`, loaded with `#load` and validated by `./build.sh check`, enables `EXT_color_buffer_float` (needed before a half float colour attachment renders), and enables the depth test (`LEQUAL`) and back face culling with counter clockwise front faces. `webgl_renderer_upload_mesh` uploads a `Mesh_Data` once into a vertex array (attribute 0 the position, attribute 1 the normal, `u32` indices) and returns its `Mesh_Id`, the index in upload order. `webgl_renderer_draw` runs two passes. The forward pass renders into a `Webgl_Target`, an `RGBA16F` colour texture (nearest filtering, clamped) with a 24 bit depth renderbuffer, which `webgl_target_ensure` recreates whenever the canvas size changes; it clears the target and issues one draw call per item of the `Draw_List`, skipping an item whose mesh or material id is out of range, and writes linear colour, so values above 1 survive. The tone map pass draws one fullscreen triangle (from `gl_VertexID`, no vertex attributes) to the canvas with the depth test off: it scales the target by the draw list's `exposure`, maps it through the ACES fit of Narkowicz 2015, `x (2.51 x + 0.03) / (x (2.43 x + 0.59) + 0.14)` clamped to 0 to 1, and applies the gamma 2.2 output. When the target is not framebuffer complete the draw only clears the canvas. The shading is physically based: a GGX specular lobe with the Smith geometry term in its Schlick-GGX form and Schlick Fresnel (`F0` 0.04 for dielectrics, the albedo for metals, mixed by `metallic`), and a Lambert diffuse term scaled by the light the Fresnel term does not reflect and by `1 - metallic`, plus the material's emissive. Roughness is floored at 0.045, where GGX would otherwise degenerate. The sun's intensity is defined so that a white Lambert surface facing it returns the intensity, so the radiance is `sun_colour * π`. The fragment shader runs at `highp`, since the squared alpha at the roughness floor sits below mediump's normal range. The constant ambient (`WEBGL_AMBIENT`, times the albedo) stays until stage 1 replaces it with the sky ambient. The sun direction is the direction the light travels, towards the scene, so a sun overhead points down. The normal is transformed with `mat3(model)`, which assumes a uniform scale in the model matrix.
