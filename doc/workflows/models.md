# The modelling workflow

The third asset workflow (definition.md, lesson 3, pipeline 5): how a machine's finished model is made from its concept turnaround, and how it is judged. Decided 2026-10-18 with item 0038, the research behind it in `doc/log/2026-10-18.md`.

## The pipeline

The accepted turnaround (front, left, back, right) goes to Tripo's multiview to model API (`v3.1-20260211`) with `generate_parts = true`, `texture = false`, `pbr = false` and a fixed `model_seed`, which returns segmented, untextured geometry: the model carries no baked look, as DESIGN.md's consistency rule wants, so nothing has to be stripped. A headless Blender script then does what no generator does: the scale, the part mapping to the brief, the cleanup, the UVs, the masks, the cue pivots and sockets, the export. A segmentation task (`/v3/mesh/segment` with the hero as the reference image) is the fallback when the parts come back wrong.

What was not verified: no generator output of a squat drum machine was seen, and hard surface rankings come from vendor blogs. The smelter is the test. Soft edges send it to Tripo's retopology or to Meshy on the same turnaround. In the first set Tripo's reductions to about ten thousand triangles and Blender's collapse to that count were unacceptable beside the full model, only the smart low poly one somewhat acceptable, and the second set at 700000 and 24000 stands with it on the models page, in three clusters, the base models in a row, Tripo's low poly ones in a column per technique and Blender's decimations in a grid of base model by target.

## Files

- `work/models/<id>/`: the generated GLB with a sidecar (task id, seed, versions, cost), untracked.
- `data/models/<id>.glb`: the finished model, glTF 2.0, metres, Y up, through Git LFS, with `data/models/<id>.sjson` beside it (the source task, seeds, generator versions, the triangle count). It holds one node and mesh per part named by the part, each part's material named by its library preset id with no textures, `TEXCOORD_0`, `COLOR_0` with R curvature, G ambient occlusion, B the emissive mask, A reserved for a wear bias, node extras `{ part, material, cue }`, pivot nodes for the cues with `{ cue, axis, range_deg }`, and socket empties with `{ socket, cue }`. The engine decides how a cue drives these; the model only names them.
- `data/demos/<demo>/meshes/<name>.mesh`: the engine's flat mesh for a demo page, made from a GLB by `tools/models/mesh_blob.py <glb> <out.mesh> [--height <metres>]` with the bottom centre of the bounding box at the origin, through Git LFS. The format: the magic `BAMESH01`, u32 vertex count, u32 index count, per vertex six little endian f32 (position then normal), then the indices as u32.

## The steps

1. `tools/models/generate.py <id> [--seed 7] [--face-limit <n>] [--smart-low-poly] [--dry-run]`: the multiview request with the four kept views of the turnaround under `data/art/<id>/turnaround/`, the poll, the download to `work/models/<id>/model_<seed>.glb` with its sidecar `model_<seed>.txt`. `--face-limit` and `--smart-low-poly` ask for Tripo's own reduction, compared with Blender's decimation on the review sheet, and name the output `model_<seed>_faces<n>_smart.glb` with each suffix present only when its flag is given. With `--smart-low-poly` the API caps `--face-limit` at 20000 (HTTP 400 above it, nothing charged). The sidecar's credits line is the balance before minus after, so two runs at once each record the sum. `--dry-run` prints the views, the request body and the output path and sends nothing. The comparison with Blender's own reduction is `tools/models/decimate.py <in.glb> <out.glb> --triangles <n>` run under `flatpak run org.blender.Blender -b --python "$PWD/tools/models/decimate.py" --`, with the output beside the Tripo variants. It welds each part first (vertices closer than 0.01 mm merge), since a generated mesh is split at its seams into unconnected patches that the collapse would crack apart, and shades the parts smooth by angle after the decimate, an edge sharper than 30 degrees kept split, since Blender exports a flat shaded mesh as three vertices per triangle. Before the decimation a model can be closed by one of four techniques. Fill holes caps every open boundary of each part with faces and triangulates them, `tools/models/close.py <in.glb> <out.glb> --technique fill` under Blender like `decimate.py`. Voxel remesh joins the parts and rebuilds them as one closed shell on a voxel grid of 0.01 units (one percent of the model's height), `close.py --technique voxel`. Screened Poisson reconstruction fits one smooth watertight surface through the merged parts at octree depth 9, `tools/models/reconstruct.py <in.glb> <out.ply> --technique poisson` (MeshLab through `uv` and pymeshlab, the shebang runs it). Alpha wrap shrinks a shell around the merged parts with alpha at one percent and offset at 0.1 percent of the bounding box diagonal, `reconstruct.py --technique alphawrap`. `decimate.py` takes the PLY of the MeshLab techniques as input as well as a GLB. `tools/models/close_all.sh [--dry-run]` makes every technique's variant of the models page from the sources under `work/models/smelter/`, the closed meshes and their decimations under `work/models/smelter/closed/`, the blobs as `data/demos/models/meshes/<blob>_<technique>.mesh`, and skips a step whose output exists, so a rerun continues.
2. `flatpak run org.blender.Blender -b --python "$PWD/tools/models/finish.py" -- <id>`: import, apply transforms, scale to the brief's height with the origin at the footprint's bottom centre, map the generated parts to the brief's parts (by the brief's blockout boxes, as `python3 -I tools/models/parts.py <id>` does from `data/machines/<id>/generated.sjson`: the source GLB, the generated axis that becomes the brief's front, the margin in metres a vertex may lie outside a box, and an override table from generated part name to brief part name; each part goes to its override, else to the brief part holding the largest share of its vertices, equal shares to the smaller blockout, else to the brief part with the nearest box centre. Before the merge the tool repairs the model as data: each of `extractions` (`near`, a point in metres in the brief's frame, and `facing`, the face's outward axis) cuts a screw the generator fused into the body or the skirt out into the fittings as `screw_<n>`, and each of `additions` (`near`, `facing` and `copy`, an index into `extractions`) fills an empty screw hole with a copy of that screw, turned by quarter turns to its facing, as `addition_<n>`. A spot's axis runs through `near` along the facing, so a `near` point must lie within about 2 mm of the hole's axis, corrected by hand from a shot when a pick misses. Its panel level is the median height of the host's vertices 13 to 16 mm from the axis. An extraction's profile is the median height per 1 mm ring out to 16 mm: the head top is the median within 3 mm, the floor the lowest ring beyond 4 mm, the half radius the first ring below the midpoint of the two, and the cut, halfway between the half radius and the floor, takes the host's triangles whose centroid lies within it. A head less than 2 mm above its floor is an error. A copy moves across the face from its source's `near` to its own and along the facing by the difference of the two panel levels. Only vertices within 25 mm of `near` along the facing count, so the far side of the part stays out. The tool prints the mapping and one line per repair and writes one blob per brief part to `data/demos/parts/meshes/<part>.mesh` in the brief's frame. Each brief part is then closed and decimated on its own, so the gaps between the parts stay open and every part keeps its material: `tools/models/finish_parts.sh <id> --trim <metres> <total> [<total> ...]` writes the merged parts as PLY under `work/models/<id>/parts/` (`parts.py --ply-directory`) and closes each part by its closing in `generated.sjson` (`closing`, brief part to `poisson`, the default, or `none`, listed by `parts.py <id> --closings`). A `poisson` part is closed by screened Poisson at a cell of 2.5 mm (`reconstruct.py --cell`, which picks the octree depth from the part's largest extent, clamped to 4..10) and trimmed of what lies farther than the trim from its own surface (`reconstruct.py --trim`: the surface Poisson spreads over an open side goes, with the pieces left under 100 faces, while holes up to about twice the trim across stay closed). A `none` part keeps its raw mesh: Poisson on the small parts left lips past them even at a 1 cm trim, so the smelter's fittings, vents and louvre are decimated only. The tool shares each total out by these face counts with at least 24 triangles a part, decimates each part to its share with `decimate.py` and writes `data/demos/parts/meshes/<part>_<total>_trim<mm>.mesh` in the brief's frame with `mesh_blob.py --keep-frame`, a `none` part's blob with the trim in its name too, since its share depends on the others. Like `close_all.sh` it skips a step whose output exists and prints each part's depth, closed and trimmed counts and budget), merge by distance, delete loose geometry, recalculate normals, weighted normals, decimate to the brief's budget, cut the lid by a plane at the brief's height when it was not separated and set its origin on the hinge, smart project and pack the UVs per part, bake ambient occlusion and curvature to colour attributes, the emissive mask from the part role graded by the concept's glow, the socket empties, the export.
3. The generated variants are looked at on the models page (`src/demos/models/`, the blobs under `data/demos/models/meshes/`) before the review sheet exists. Review sheet: `work/models/<id>_review.png`, the hero and four orthographic renders in library materials under the material sheet's light beside the concept at the same camera, the figure, the wireframe, the parts and each mask in false colour, and the idle, working and venting frames. The checks: the bounding box against the footprint and height within a few percent, the triangles within budget, every brief part present with an existing library preset, no non manifold or loose geometry, UVs inside 0 to 1 without overlap, the mask ranges, the silhouette overlap of the orthographic renders against the turnaround, a pivot or socket for every cue of the brief. The user's eye decides.

## Rejected

- A scripted kit of primitives as the finished model: crisp edges and exact parts, and the programmer art lesson 1 forbids. It stays the blockout and the placeholder.
- Meshy: its parts mode is the older topology option, no multiview on its API page, the licence unread. Kept as the comparison for the smelter if its edges come back soft.
- Hyper3D Rodin: cited as the best at hard surface, the API and commercial licence behind a monthly business plan.
- TRELLIS.2: one shell with a baked look. Hunyuan3D: open weights whose licence excludes the EU.
- Blender MCP servers: an interactive viewport, nothing over the headless scripts the project already drives.
- CC0 kits (Kenney, Poly Haven): a cartoon low poly style or photoscans, neither the look; reference only.
