# The art book workflow

The second asset workflow (definition.md, lesson 3, pipeline 1): how a machine's concept images are made from its brief, and how they are judged. Decided 2026-10-18 with item 0038, the research behind it in `doc/log/2026-10-18.md`.

## The pipeline

A machine starts as a brief. A Blender script builds a blockout from it, primitives of the right size in library materials, and renders it from the hero camera and in four orthographic views. An image model then repaints those renders into concept art, with the render as the reference image. The control comes from a flaw Mine oh Belowed's booklet found in repaints (`~/dev/mine-oh-belowed/doc/art/booklet.md`, How the images are made): a repaint with a reference keeps the reference's composition and geometry. Here that is the point. The blockout fixes silhouette, proportion and footprint, and its orthographic renders give a true turnaround, which text prompts alone never gave. A text only round comes first only when the silhouette itself is open.

The image model runs through OpenRouter (the key at `~/.config/openrouter/key`) with `tools/art/generate.py`, a copy of Mine oh Belowed's tool (references, seeds, a cost log). The default model is Google's Nano Banana Pro; the first machine runs GPT Image 2.5 beside it, and from then on each family has one fixed model and its accepted hero image as the style reference. Prompts are composed from the brief and the family's style paragraph, never appended by hand (the global rule on image prompts).

## Files

- `data/machines/<id>/brief.sjson`: `id`, `family`, `role`, `footprint = { cells, clearance, cell_metres }`, `height_metres`, `figure_metres`, `entry` (the entry's clause: setting, state, colours), `silhouette` (one sentence), `feature` (the one readable feature), `feature_edge_on` (the feature as a side elevation shows it), `parts` (name, library material, role, and a `blockout`: one shape or a list of shapes, `box`, `cylinder` or `dome`, each with an optional `bevel = { width, segments }`, a `box` also an optional `taper` (the top face as a factor of the bottom) and `corner_cut` (metres chamfered off each vertical edge)), `cues` (name, the behaviour in words, the parts, sockets and pivot it needs), `art = { model, seeds }`. The brief drives the blockout, the prompts and later the model's finishing.
- `data/art/families.sjson`: one style paragraph per machine family, composed into every prompt of the family, and the family's `palette` for the review sheet.
- `data/art/book.sjson`: the art book's `pitch`, `figure` (the astronaut for scale) and `presentation` (a hand painted illustration, no page, no lettering), written once for the game, composed around each brief's `entry` in the entry round.
- `data/art/page.png`: the empty page of the book, its hero frame over seven tenths of the paper and the elevation boxes and the swatches in a strip below, laid out by `tools/art/page_template.py layout` from the model's passes (its prompts and the layout in `data/art/page.txt`). Kept, no longer referenced: the entry round paints without the page since the frame took half the picture and the lettering leaked (user, 2026-10-18). `data/art/figure.png`: the astronaut alone, the player character's first portrait (its prompts in `data/art/figure.txt`), not on the page: the character gets its own design, so the figure for scale is asked for in words.
- `work/art/<id>/`: the rounds, each image beside its prompt file, untracked (DESIGN.md, Working files).
- `data/art/<id>/`: the kept hero and turnaround with their prompt sidecars, through Git LFS. Whether these pages ship in the game is still the open call of DESIGN.md.

## The steps

0. Entry round, when the machine's look is open: `tools/art/concept.py <id> --entry --round <n> [--seeds 7 11]` paints the machine as a hand painted concept illustration in its setting beside the astronaut, no page, no lettering, no reference image; the pictures land in `work/art/<id>/entry-<n>/`, on Banana (GPT drops the lettering and paints photographic grime, the 2026-10-18 trial). The machine's height is stated against the figure ("reaches up to torso height of the astronaut", from the brief's metres), the one size the models draw: a number in metres is overshot by about half, whether as words or as 1x1x1 m. The chosen entry's parts and proportions are read into the brief and its colours into the family's palette.
1. Write or edit the brief.
2. Blockout: `flatpak run org.blender.Blender -b --python "$PWD/tools/art/blockout.py" -- <id>` renders `hero.png`, `front.png`, `left.png`, `back.png`, `right.png` and a silhouette mask of each into `work/art/<id>/blockout/`, each framing the machine with a small margin and the four elevations at one scale.
3. Repaint: `tools/art/concept.py <id> --round <n> [--model <name>] [--seeds 7 11 19]` sends the hero render as the reference with the prompt composed from the brief and the family style, two or three seeds per model, into `work/art/<id>/round-<n>/`. No figure stands in the blockout: the scale is the entry's, and an astronaut painted into the hero walked into the turnaround's right view as a bust three times (round 7). Naming the fire brought thin ink outlines (round 5). "Painted edges, no ink lines" swung the repaint to a photoreal render that lost the blockout's taper and vents (round 6), while "painterly, soft brushed edges, forms set apart by value and colour, never by lines, not a 3D render" kept the blockout and dropped the lines (round 7). The user picks the hero. The round's default form is the collage: `tools/art/concept.py <id> --collage --round <n> [--seeds 7]` puts the five renders on a white three by two sheet, `round-<n>/collage/sheet.png`, repaints all five in one call with one material set for the price of one view, and splits the output into `hero.png`, `front.png`, `left.png`, `back.png` and `right.png` of cut at about 840 px and resized to 1024 for the masks. The sheet goes out at its own 1536 px, since the reference downscale to 1024 px shrank the skirt vents to 10 px and the louvre slats to 8 px and the model dropped both.
4. Turnaround, kept since the collage for a single view's rerun: `tools/art/concept.py <id> --turnaround --round <n> [--views front back]` repaints each orthographic render, its one reference, into `work/art/<id>/round-<n>/turnaround/`, a failed view rerun on its own. The prompt is the hero's with the view named as a flat orthographic elevation without perspective and the feature told per view: the brief's `feature` on the front, `feature_edge_on` and the side the front face is on for the left and right, the front turned away for the back. With the hero as a second reference its composition leaked into the side views (an astronaut, a mirrored side, the hero's body ghosted above the elevation, five right views failed in a row), and with the hero's figure sentence Banana painted the hero's three quarter view four times out of four (smelter round 4, 2026-10-19). The render alone with the hero's wording painted a clean elevation whose materials came from the style (round 8), and "on the front" put the hearth mouth on the side face, hence the feature per view. The family style names no figure: its "an astronaut figure beside it for scale" put a bust in the corner of a right view (round 7). The model still scales a view up or shifts it (front silhouette 0.44), which the sheet shows and the eye forgives. The cue states (idle, working) as edits of the hero when the brief has them.
5. Review sheet: `tools/art/sheet.py <id> --round <n>` writes `round-<n>/review.png`: blockout, repaints, turnaround, a 64 pixel greyscale squint row, the height line. Per repaint against its mask, read from edges so horizon and shadows do not count: `SILHOUETTE` the share of the mask outline with an image edge near (ok above 0.85), `PALETTE` masked share near the family `palette` (above 0.6), `HEIGHT` the signed offset of the top edge from the mask's top over its height (within 0.05). The user's eye decides; the kept images go to `data/art/<id>/`.

## Rejected

- Text to image alone, as Mine oh Belowed did: good for exploring, no control of scale or views, every machine drifting in proportion.
- Midjourney: no public API.
- The blockout render as the finished art: programmer art, lesson 1. It is the control image and the placeholder.
